    The Grandmother¶

    "I've been eating the dark for thousands of years."

    Overview¶
    Name Unknown — called the Grandmother, the Memory Eater of the Seven Cedars, the Dream Eater
    Type Nature spirit — the spirit of the grove, cast into service long ago by parties unknown (GM retcon after Session Fourteen — see note below)
    Location Formerly beneath the basin of the Hollow of Seven Cedars, in the interwoven root system of all seven trees
    Status Freed — and departed. Unbound by the party's reversal (Session Fourteen); struck at her own request, she handed Ginkgo a curse-cleansing seed and dissipated entirely

    GM retcon (post–Session Fourteen)

    Gary's post-session note corrected her nature: the Grandmother was a nature spirit of Tian Xia's active spirit realm — the spirit of the grove, cast into service long ago. By whom and why, the party may yet find out. This supersedes the vaguer "ancient grove spirit (occult)" framing, and it reframes her seed: a spirit's gift that another spirit can receive. The seed can be offered to the spirit of Willowshore to cleanse the whole town — see Willowshore.

    Description¶

    The Grandmother is the spirit of the Hollow of Seven Cedars — the entity pilgrims petitioned for eons at the Lovers' Grove. Her domain is the vast interweaving of roots beneath the stone basin, connecting all seven cedars; when the drained basin cracked and vented in Session Twelve, the party glimpsed that root-lattice through the fissures, and something almost like a face in the shadows — "like seeing an elephant in the clouds."

    Only Ginkgo can hear her. Having challenged the pool and passed its test in Session Eleven, he is now the speaker of the pool; her voice reaches him through the mycelial groundswell — quiet, tired, wavery, elderly.

    Her ancient function was subtractive mercy: pilgrims cast their darkest memories into her pool, and she ate the darkness, poisoning herself in their place so the living could heal. She has done this, by her own account, for thousands of years.

    The Binding¶

    Kurosawa's Root Correction (the Second Key) — the geomantic rite behind the copper nails and the grafted root — did not merely corrupt the grove. It bound the Grandmother and inverted her: for nearly a year she has been forced to eat the good memories of Willowshore's people, funneled down the ley line, changing the town's history by changing what it remembers. She does not know why he wants this; she only knows what her stolen gift is being made to do.

    She is powerless to act beyond what the ritual compels — unable to speak to anyone but the pool's speaker, unable to reach her "allies in the woods."

    Critically, she did not send the Warden. The seal corrupted the whole grove, Warden included; the same crowding voices Ginkgo felt puppeting the creature are the seal(s) restricting her.

    What She Offers¶

    Spoken to Ginkgo across the cracked basin (Session Twelve):

    • The grove does not have to die. If the party reverses the Root Correction — its steps performed in reverse order, "by voice and by actions," giving back the name and undoing the seals — she can "open my mouth again... and eat the darkness." The originator is not required
    • What is lost is lost. "What went downhill must remain downhill." The good memories already eaten from Willowshore cannot be returned; the townsfolk "will not remember you kindly." Reversal stops the infection; it does not cure the wound
    • After she is freed: eyes and ears in the woods, such allies as she has — but "no army and no hero"
    • She knows the ritual by its shape and diagram, not its maker; she cannot say who Kurosawa is, only what his working did

    Ginkgo probed her for deception (Perception 23) and found none — only the same restriction, the same seal, pressing on her voice.

    The Seed (the Plum)¶

    Her parting gift, pressed warm into Ginkgo's hand as she faded (Session Fourteen): a black, walnut-like seed shaped like a Mourndusk Willow seed but larger and softer — the party immediately dubbed it the plum. It is not natural at all (occult/divine essence in a seed's shape; planted, it grows nothing). Her instruction:

    "This seed will clear the curse of one in Willowshore. They must consume it, but it will return their mind unblemished."

    Half was fed to Radiant Willow, curing her completely and instantly. Per the GM's retcon, the seed could also be given to the spirit of Willowshore — commune, earn its favor, and the town spirit consumes it and cleanses everyone. Half remains, and the party's choice stands between one more person and the harder, larger road.

    Open Questions¶

    • Who cast her into service, and why? The retcon dangles this explicitly: she was a nature spirit pressed into the Dream Eater's role long ago. Her jailer — and their purpose — is an open thread
    • Where did she go? Her presence dissipated entirely when the grove healed. Destroyed, released, or returned to the spirit realm?
    • Was the strike she asked for a mercy or a necessity? "Do not cut the trees. Cut me. It is the same kindness, only smaller" — the ritual required it, but her phrasing suggests she understood it as the same gift she once gave pilgrims
    • The second seal remains unexplained. Her "nearly a year" binding matched the older erasure over Cassian Voss's name better than Kurosawa's nails — yet reversing Kurosawa's rite alone freed her. Voss's parting message ("it was not the oni") says the other hand is still out there

    Session History¶

    Session Eleven (2026-06-04)¶

    • Her legend recovered (Littlefinger's Society 23): the Dream Eater who ate pilgrims' dark memories and poisoned herself in their place
    • Her pool tested Ginkgo ("who would you save?"); his answer drained the basin and made him the pool's speaker

    Session Twelve (2026-07-16)¶

    • Spoke for the first time — through the cracked, venting basin, to Ginkgo alone, after the Warden's death
    • Revealed she has been bound nearly a year and inverted — forced to eat Willowshore's good memories down the ley line
    • Named the binding rite: the Root Correction, the Second Key — and the terms of its reversal (steps in reverse, voice and action, give back the name, undo the seals; originator not required)
    • Confirmed the grove need not die — overturning Session Eleven's verdict — but that Willowshore's stolen memories are gone for good
    • Denied sending the Warden; passed Ginkgo's deception read; promised eyes and ears in the woods once freed

    Session Thirteen (2026-08-13)¶

    • Her unbinding began. Donkey's analysis of the stolen ritual produced the full five-step reversal, ending with her release: once the seals unlock, "she will start eating things backwards" — consuming the black ichor back in
    • Da Baishan's Arcana (24) confirmed a requirement her conversation with Ginkgo implied: someone must stand in the pool for the final step, and it should be the one who entered the water originally — Ginkgo, her speaker, volunteered without hesitation
    • At session's end, Littlefinger's third counterclockwise circuit of the seal raised the ichor as smoke, coalescing into a shadowy shape above her basin — whether her, her jailers, or something else entirely is next session's first question

    Session Fourteen (2026-08-20)¶

    • Freed. With all five reversal steps complete, she rose from the basin as a rough, blackened-marble silhouette of an elderly woman, pulling the corruption back in — the ley line's poison reeled away from Willowshore in a tug-of-war she was winning
    • Asked Ginkgo, the pacifist standing in her pool, to finish the ritual by striking her: "Do not cut the trees. Cut me. It is the same kindness, only smaller." He did — a Divine Lance of "life force," accepting "the consequences of my deity"
    • As she faded, she reached out an open hand with the seed — see The Seed (the Plum) above — warm to the touch, with her instruction to feed it to one of Willowshore's cursed
    • The grove healed in minutes — bark brightening, leaves greening, clean water refilling her basin — and her presence vanished entirely. The Dream Eater no longer lives at the Hollow of Seven Cedars
    • Post-session retcon: reclassified as a nature spirit cast into service long ago — see the note at the top of this page
